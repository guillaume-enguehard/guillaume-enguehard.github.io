The rules in one minute

Each side has 8 circles, 4 triangles and 4 squares carrying the numbers 2, 3, 4 and 6, set up as in medieval rithmomachia: squares on the wings, triangles in the centre, circles in front. White moves first.

Goal: the triumph

You win by lining up three of your pieces side by side (in a row, a column or a diagonal), in the opponent's half of the board, whose numbers form a progression: 2-3-4, 2-4-6, 2-3-6 or 3-4-6, in either direction. The middle number is always 3 or 4.

A player left with fewer than three different numbers can no longer triumph and loses. A player with no legal move loses. After 60 plies without a capture, the game is drawn.

Movement

In a straight line, in all 8 directions, without jumping: the circle 1 square, the triangle 1 or 2, the square 1, 2 or 3.

The three captures

  1. Encounter (equality): land on an enemy piece with the same number but a different shape. Two identical pieces cannot capture each other.
  2. Assault (multiplication or division): land on an enemy piece worth double or half your number after travelling exactly 2 squares, triple or a third after exactly 3.
  3. Ambush (addition or subtraction): after your move, the piece you moved and another friendly piece frame an enemy piece on a line; if the sum or difference of their two numbers equals its number, it is captured.

Capturing is never compulsory: you capture to open a way, to defend, or to deprive your opponent of a number.

On the board

Tap a piece: dots show its moves, red rings the moves that capture. Hovering a ring circles the pieces that would fall. The dotted line marks the middle of the board. Under each side, the numbers it still owns: a crossed-out number is lost.

“Undo” takes back one move; against the computer, it goes back to your last move.
